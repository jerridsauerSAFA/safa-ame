    Swap seats one more time. The new pilot gets 60 seconds to break the aircraft; the recorder has to explain, out loud, why it broke.

    Simplified model for teaching. The CL curve, stall angle (≈ 16°, ≈ 14° with flaps), centre-of-pressure travel, drag coefficients (CD0 0.03, AR 7.5, e 0.8) and the pitch response are typical values for a light training aircraft, not numbers for a specific type — confirm real figures against the aircraft manual. Atmosphere is ISA. Sources: FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25C), ch. 4–5; NASA Glenn Research Center, "Incorrect Lift Theory"; Transport Canada Flight Training Manual (TP 1102), Exercises 3 and 12; SAIT ASYS 245 Module 2 Lift & Drag; From the Ground Up, Theory of Flight.

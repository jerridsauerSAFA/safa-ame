Lift & drag

Lift equation

Lift

L = CL · ½ρV² · A
Llift (N or lb)CLlift coefficient — shape + angle of attackρair densityVvelocityAwing area

Bigger wing, denser air, more angle of attack, and above all more speed → more lift.

½ρV² is dynamic pressure — the same thing the airspeed indicator measures.

Worked example

CL = 0.5, ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, V = 50 m/s, A = 16 m²: L = 0.5 × 0.5 × 1.225 × 50² × 16 = 12,250 N (about 1,250 kg of aircraft). At 100 m/s the same wing makes 49,000 N — four times as much.

L = 12250 N
Drag equation

Drag

D = CD · ½ρV² · A
DdragCDdrag coefficient — found in a wind tunnelρ, V, Aas for lift

Same shape as the lift equation. Drag also grows with the square of speed.

CD bundles form, skin friction and induced drag. Rearranged: CD = D ÷ (½ρV²A) — measure D, work backwards.

Worked example

CD = 0.03, same air, V = 50 m/s, A = 16 m²: D = 0.03 × 0.5 × 1.225 × 2500 × 16 = 735 N. Lift ÷ drag = 12,250 ÷ 735 ≈ 16.7 — the lift-to-drag ratio.

D = 735 N
Newton's second law

Force = mass × acceleration

F = m × a
Fforcemmass of air movedahow fast its velocity changes (ΔV ÷ Δt)

The wing pushes air down; the air pushes the wing up by the same amount.

Written for airflow: F = m × ΔV ÷ Δt. Move more air, or change its velocity faster, and you get more lift.

Worked example

A wing deflects 500 kg of air per second downward by 25 m/s: F = 500 × 25 = 12,500 N of lift — the same order as the lift-equation example above.

F = 12500 N

Wing shape & efficiency

Aspect ratio

Span ÷ Chord

AR = span ÷ chord
spanwingtip to wingtipchordaverage front-to-back width

Long and narrow = high aspect ratio = less induced drag for the same lift.

Gliders ≈ 20–30. Cessna 172 ≈ 7.3. Fighters ≈ 2–4. (For a tapered wing engineers use span² ÷ area — same idea.)

Worked example

Cessna 172: span 11.0 m, wing area 16.2 m² → average chord 1.47 m → AR = 11.0 ÷ 1.47 ≈ 7.5.

AR = 7.5
Lift-to-drag ratio

Efficiency

L / D
LliftDtotal drag at that speed

How many units of lift you get for each unit of drag. Higher = more efficient.

Best L/D is at the bottom of the total-drag U-curve, where parasite drag = induced drag. Also the best glide ratio (Module 5).

Worked example

L = 12,250 N, D = 735 N → L/D = 16.7. A glider might reach 40+; an airliner in cruise about 17–19.

L/D = 16.7
Speed scaling

The V² rule

L₂ = L₁ × (V₂ ÷ V₁)²
L₁, V₁lift and speed nowL₂, V₂lift and speed after the change

Change the speed by a factor, and lift (and drag) change by that factor squared.

× 2 speed → × 4 lift. × 1.5 speed → × 2.25. × 0.7 speed → about half.

Worked example

Lift is 10,000 N at 60 kt. At 90 kt (1.5×): 10,000 × 1.5² = 22,500 N — so the pilot must lower the angle of attack to keep level flight.

L₂ = 22500

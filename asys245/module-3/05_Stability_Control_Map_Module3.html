3

Control vs stability

Flight control = control
  • Moving surface (aileron, elevator, rudder)
  • The pilot works it
  • Makes the aircraft move
Stabilizer = stability
  • Fixed part or shape (wings, h. stab, fin)
  • Works on its own, no pilot input
  • Brings it back after a disturbance
Stability is behaviour, not an axis. The names cross over: lateral stability = roll (longitudinal axis); longitudinal stability = pitch (lateral axis). Yaw stability is called directional, not "vertical."
4

Wing shape and roll stability

Dihedralwings angle up → adds roll stability. Most light aircraft.
Flatno angle → the wing shape adds no roll stability by itself.
Anhedralwings angle down → reduces roll stability, more agile. Often high-wing aircraft that already have plenty.
5

Where the CG sits

Too far forward
  • Nose heavy
  • Tail must push down harder → higher stall speed
  • Elevator may run out: hard to lift off or flare
Inside the CG range
  • Set by the manufacturer (forward + aft limit)
  • Load inside it every flight
  • Stable and controllable
Too far aft
  • Tail heavy, too close to the centre of lift
  • Unstable in pitch
  • Hard to recover from a stall; a spin can go flat (most dangerous)
Side to side
  • Uneven fuel or load → one wing heavy
  • Pilot holds aileron or trim → extra drag
  • Helicopters are hit hardest
Can CG cause a stall? No. Too much angle of attack causes a stall. But CG changes the stall: forward raises the stall speed; aft makes the stall and spin harder to recover from. Rule: forward = stable but heavy · aft = light but unstable.

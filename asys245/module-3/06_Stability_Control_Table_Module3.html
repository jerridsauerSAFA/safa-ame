AStability on each axis · stability = how the aircraft behaves on its own
Lateral stabilitystable in roll Longitudinal axis (tip to tail) · roll: wings tilt left ↔ right Ailerons Wings: dihedral; also high wing (keel / pendulum effect) and sweepback A wing drops → sideslip → the low wing meets the air at a higher angle of attack → more lift → rolls level + Wings level themselves hands-off; easier for training and cloud flying Too much: fights the pilot in turns; feeds Dutch roll. Too little: spiral tendency. Anhedral (wings down) reduces it Dihedral: Piper PA-28 (low wing) · High wing: Cessna 172 · Anhedral: C-17, BAe 146, Harrier
Longitudinal stabilitystable in pitch Lateral axis (wingtip to wingtip) · pitch: nose up ↕ down Elevator (or a stabilator / canard) Horizontal stabilizer + CG ahead of the centre of lift The wing pitches the nose down; the tail pushes down to balance it, like a seesaw + Holds its speed and attitude; nose returns after a gust Only works with the CG in limits (see section D). The tail's down-load is extra weight the wing must carry Elevator: Cessna 172, Boeing 737 · Stabilator: Piper PA-28
Directional stabilitystable in yaw Vertical axis (right through the middle) · yaw: nose left ↔ right Rudder Vertical stabilizer (fin); sometimes an offset fin; sweepback helps Nose swings → air hits the side of the fin → pushes the tail back in line, like a weathervane + Flies straight; points into the relative wind. Offset fin cancels the propeller's spiral slipstream Too much (vs lateral): spiral instability. Too little: Dutch roll Large fins on multi-engine aircraft · offset fins on many single-engine propeller aircraft
BHow stable · both apply to all three axes
Static stabilitythe first move The initial tendency after the balance (equilibrium) is disturbed by the pilot, weather or turbulence Pilot inputs and trim Same fixed parts as section A Positive = heads back · Neutral = stays in the new position · Negative = keeps moving away + Positive: forgiving, flies hands-off. Neutral or negative: very agile More stable = slower to manoeuvre. Negative static stability needs computers to fly Positive: trainers, airliners · Relaxed (computer-flown): F-16
Dynamic stabilityover time What the motion does over time after a disturbance: does the swinging (oscillation) shrink, stay or grow? Pilot, or an automatic system (yaw damper) Same fixed parts as section A Positive = swings get smaller · Neutral = swings stay the same · Negative = swings grow + Positive: settles down by itself Negative: each swing is bigger; the pilot must stop it. Needs positive static first Designed positive static and positive dynamic: most certified aircraft
CWhen stability and control fight each other
Dutch rollroll + yaw wobble Aircraft wanders in roll and yaw at the same time Yaw damper (moves the rudder automatically) Strong dihedral effect, often from swept wings Lateral (dihedral effect) stability is larger than directional stability Not dangerous Uncomfortable; passengers feel unwell Swept-wing jets; most large aircraft carry yaw dampers
Spiral instabilityDutch roll's opposite A slow, tightening, descending turn Pilot levels the wings with the ailerons Large fin compared to dihedral Directional stability is larger than lateral stability Develops slowly; easy to correct Unnoticed (e.g. in cloud) it can steepen into a dangerous spiral dive Most light aircraft are slightly spirally unstable
Adverse yawnose goes the wrong way Roll one way → nose yaws the opposite way (vertical axis) Coordinated rudder + the 4 design fixes → Ailerons, wings, rudder. The down aileron adds camber The rising wing's down aileron makes more lift and more drag → drags that wing back None: a side effect to manage Uncoordinated, inefficient turns; more drag Worst at low speed with large aileron movement (e.g. gliders)
4 adverse-yaw fixesdesign solutions
1. Differential ailerons: the up aileron moves farther than the down one → extra drag on the descending wing.
2. Frise ailerons: the up aileron's nose dips below the wing → drag on the descending wing.
3. Aileron–rudder interconnect: springs move the rudder with the ailerons.
4. Roll spoilers: a panel rises on the descending wing → kills lift and adds drag on the side of the turn.
 + Less rudder work; more coordinated turns More parts / weight; differential and Frise reduce adverse yaw but don't remove all of it Frise + differential: Cessna 172 and most light aircraft · Interconnect: Beech Bonanza · Spoilers: Boeing 737, MU-2
DDesign and loading choices
CG positioncentre of gravity Must sit inside the CG range (forward + aft limit) set by the manufacturer How the aircraft is loaded (people, cargo, fuel) Weight & balance data; all three axes cross at the CG Forward → nose heavy. Aft → tail heavy. Side to side → one wing heavy + In range: stable and controllable Fwd: higher stall speed, hard to flare. Aft: unstable, hard stall/spin recovery. Side: hold aileron → drag Every aircraft · side-to-side matters most for helicopters
Conventional taillow / mid stabilizer Horizontal stabilizer mounted low or mid on the tail Elevator + rudder Horizontal + vertical stabilizer at the back Balances the wing's nose-down pitch; fin gives directional stability + Simple, light, proven, easy to inspect Sits in the wing's wake and prop / engine blast Cessna 172 · Boeing 737 · Airbus A320
T-tailstabilizer on top of the fin Horizontal stabilizer mounted on top of the vertical fin Elevator + rudder Stronger fin to hold the stabilizer up Tail sits out of the engine blast and the wing's wake + Better, more predictable pitch control at low speed Heavier. At very high angle of attack the wing's wake can blank the tail: deep stall Piper PA-38 Tomahawk · Bombardier CRJ · Boeing 727
Canardwing in front Small horizontal wing ahead of the main wing. Control canard (for pitch) or lifting canard (shares the weight) Canard surface (moves or has an elevator) Canard + main wing behind the CG; vertical fins (often at the wingtips) Canard lifts up instead of pushing down; designed to stall first so the nose drops + Extra lift, less drag; stall resistant; out of the wing's downwash Destabilizing; tight CG limits; hard to use big wing flaps; if the canard stalls, pitch control is lost for a moment Beechcraft Starship · Rutan VariEze · fighters (Eurofighter Typhoon)
Memory line: Roll – Longitudinal – Ailerons – Wings (lateral) · Pitch – Lateral – Elevator – Horizontal stab (longitudinal) · Yaw – Vertical – Rudder – Fin (directional). Rule: forward CG = stable but heavy · aft CG = light but unstable.

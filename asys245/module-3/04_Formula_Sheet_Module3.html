Watch the cross-over: roll happens about the longitudinal axis but is called lateral stability. Pitch happens about the lateral axis but is called longitudinal stability. Yaw is the easy one — vertical axis, directional stability.

Static stability comes first. An aircraft can be statically stable and dynamically unstable (it tries to return, but overshoots more each time). Certified aircraft must be positive in both.

The one formula

Moments

Moment = Force × Arm

M = F × d
Mmoment — the turning effect (lb·in or N·m)Fforce (lift, weight, tail load)darm — distance from the CG

A small force far from the CG turns the aircraft as much as a big force close to it. That is why the tail is small and far back.

Every stability story in this module is two moments fighting: the nose-down moment of lift behind the CG versus the nose-up moment of tail downforce. Level flight = the two are equal.

Worked example

Lift acts 4 in behind the CG on a 2,400 lb aircraft: nose-down moment 2,400 × 4 = 9,600 lb·in. The tail is 180 in behind the CG, so it only needs 9,600 ÷ 180 = 53 lb of downforce to hold the nose level.

M = 9600
Tail balance

Tail force needed

Ftail = M ÷ dtail
Mthe wing's nose-down momentdtaildistance from CG to the tail (tail arm)

Longer tail arm → less tail force needed → less drag and a more stable aircraft.

Move the CG aft and the wing's arm shrinks → less tail force needed → light, twitchy elevator. Move it too far and the tail must push up — the aircraft is now unstable.

Worked example

Same 9,600 lb·in moment. Short tail arm 120 in: 9,600 ÷ 120 = 80 lb. Long tail arm 240 in: 9,600 ÷ 240 = 40 lb. Half the force, half the trim drag.

Ftail = 53.3

Adverse yaw — the four fixes

FixHow it works
Differential aileronsThe up-going aileron travels farther than the down-going one → drag on the inside wing matches the outside.
Frise aileronsThe up-going aileron's nose pokes below the wing into the airflow, adding drag on the inside.
Aileron–rudder interconnectA spring or cable adds a little rudder automatically when ailerons move.
Spoilers / flaperonsA spoiler rises on the down-going wing — it kills lift and adds drag on the same side, so no yaw the wrong way.

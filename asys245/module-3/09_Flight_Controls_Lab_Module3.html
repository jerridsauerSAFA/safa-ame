What you are doing

Controls are centred. Move a slider and this panel explains it.

Ailerons → ROLL

AxisLongitudinal (nose to tail, through the CG) ControlLateral control HowAilerons move opposite. Down aileron adds lift on that wing; up aileron reduces it. Wing with more lift rises. Stabilized byLateral stability: wing dihedral and keel effect. Not strong; the aircraft may stay banked. Watch forAdverse yaw. The down-going aileron makes more drag, so the nose yaws away from the roll. The ball slides out. Fix it with rudder.

Elevator → PITCH

AxisLateral (wingtip to wingtip, through the CG) ControlLongitudinal control HowStick back = elevator up = tail pushed down = nose up. Changes angle of attack, not altitude directly. Stabilized byLongitudinal stability: the horizontal stabilizer and CG ahead of the centre of lift. Strongest of the three; the nose returns on its own. Watch forPull too far, too slow: angle of attack passes the critical angle and the wing stalls. Pitch controls airspeed; power controls altitude.

Rudder → YAW

AxisVertical (normal axis, top to bottom through the CG) ControlDirectional control HowRight pedal = rudder right = tail pushed left = nose right. The rudder does not turn the aircraft; bank does. Rudder keeps the turn coordinated. Stabilized byDirectional stability: the vertical fin acts like a weathervane. Yaw stops when you release the pedal, but heading does not return. Watch forRudder alone also makes the aircraft roll a little (the outside wing speeds up). That is why controls are used together.

Flaps → no axis

TypeSecondary control. Changes the wing's camber: more lift and more drag at the same speed. UseSlower approach, steeper descent, shorter takeoff. Flaps change performance, not attitude. Expect a small pitch change when they move.
Try this · 15 minutes · work in pairs
  1. 1. One control at a timeMove only the ailerons. Write the axis and the motion. Centre. Repeat for elevator, then rudder. Three axes, three motions.
  2. 2. Find adverse yawRoll right with ailerons only. Watch the heading and the ball. Which way did the nose go? Now hold a little right rudder while rolling. Keep the ball in the middle.
  3. 3. Which one comes back?Pitch up 20°, centre, wait. Bank 30°, centre, wait. Yaw 30°, centre, wait. Rank the three axes from most to least self-correcting. Name the part that does each job.
  4. 4. Kill the stabilizersUntick "Stabilizers active" and repeat task 3. What changed? Explain why a tailless aircraft is hard to fly.
  5. 5. Coordinated turnEnter a 30° left bank, ball centred, then hold it. Release ailerons once banked. What keeps the aircraft turning? Write a one-line rule a new pilot could use.

Memory hook: the motion and the control type share a name with the other axis. Roll happens about the longitudinal axis but is called lateral control. Pitch happens about the lateral axis but is called longitudinal control. All three axes cross at the centre of gravity.

QuestionAnswer
Who does the empty W&B?The AME
Who does the loaded W&B?The pilot

Weighing checklist

StepDo
1. ReadManufacturer's weighing procedure, datum, levelling means, arms to weighing points (AMM / TCDS)
2. ConfigureFlaps/spoilers/slats per manual · equipment list items installed · baggage empty · panels, caps, doors fitted · hydraulics full · water and lav drained
3. Fuel & oilNo fuel (add unusable back) · residual only · or full (subtract usable, hydrometer for density). Oil per TCDS
4. ConditionsClosed hangar, fans off, no air currents; aircraft clean and dry
5. LevelSpirit level on the levelling means or plumb bob; at least 3 scales / load cells
6. ReadScale weights; weigh tare items (chocks, blocks) and subtract
7. TableItem · weight · arm · moment → totals → CG = ΣM ÷ ΣW; useful load = max gross − empty
8. Check & recordCG between the weighing points? Inside the envelope? Build the report, equipment list, sign

Four calculators

The core formula

CG from a table

CG = ΣM ÷ ΣW
ΣMsum of all moments (weight × arm, signs included)ΣWsum of all weights (removed items negative)

Three weighing points plus one correction row. Enter the scale readings and arms; the moments and CG update live.

Default = the deck's practice aircraft (nose wheel assembly and unusable fuel already added, oil removed as a negative row).

Worked example

Nose 331 @ −10 = −3,310 · R main 423 @ 46.81 = 19,800.6 · L main 415 @ 46.81 = 19,426.2 · corrections (oil −13.13 @ −14.7, fuel +9 @ 40, nose wheel +11.5 @ −10) net +7.37 lb and +438.0 lb·in → 36,354.8 ÷ 1,176.37 = 30.90 in.

ΣW 1,176.37 lb · ΣM 36,354.8 lb·in
CG = 30.90 in · useful load 493.63 lb
After a change

Amendment

new CG = (M₀ + ΣΔM) ÷ (W₀ + ΣΔW)
W₀, CG₀previous report's empty weight and CG (M₀ = W₀ × CG₀)ΔWeach item: + installed, − removed

Start from the previous report, add the installed items, subtract the removed ones, divide. No scales needed when every weight and arm is known.

Default = the float installation: 1,250 lb @ 27.2; remove gear 132 @ 20 and tailwheel 20 @ 200; add floats 308 @ 24.

Worked example

34,000 − 2,640 − 4,000 + 7,392 = 34,752 lb·in; 1,250 − 132 − 20 + 308 = 1,406 lb; 34,752 ÷ 1,406 = 24.72 in. CG moved forward about 2.5 in.

New weight 1,406 lb · new CG 24.72 in · moved 2.48 fwd
Fixing the CG

Ballast placement

arm = moment ÷ weight
momentthe moment you must cancel (equal and opposite)weightthe ballast you havearmwhere it must go, from the datum

Pick the weight and solve for the arm — or pick a spot (arm) and solve for the weight: weight = moment ÷ arm. A longer arm does more per pound.

Deck example: net moment +550 lb·in, 50 lb weight → −11 in (11 in forward of the datum). Check: 50 × −11 = −550.

Worked example

Need to move the moment by −550 with 50 lb: −550 ÷ 50 = −11 in. With only 25 lb it would need −22 in; at 5 in from the datum you would need −550 ÷ −5 = 110 lb.

Place it at arm −11.0 in
needs 22.0 lb
Large aircraft

CG as % MAC

% MAC = (CG − LEMAC) ÷ MAC × 100
LEMAC / TEMACleading / trailing edge of the mean aerodynamic chord, from the datum (TCDS)MACTEMAC − LEMAC

Business jets and airliners state the CG range as a percentage of the MAC so loading passengers and cargo works the same on every aircraft of the type.

Deck example: 78 in chord, CG range 15.6–28.08 in aft of the leading edge = 20–36 % MAC.

Worked example

LEMAC 100 in, TEMAC 178 in → MAC = 78. A CG at 123.4 in: (123.4 − 100) ÷ 78 × 100 = 30 % MAC.

MAC = 78.0 in · CG = 28.0 % MAC

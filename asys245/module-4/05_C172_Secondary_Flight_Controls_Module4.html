Secondary Flight Controls

Five lift-and-drag devices in position on a 172 silhouette. A stock Skyhawk carries flaps only; slats, slots, spoilers and speed brakes are drawn where other aircraft mount them.

Flaps Slats Slots Spoilers Speed brakes Primary controls (grey)
What each one is for
ControlPrimary purposeMost often usedFound on
Flapstrailing edge, inboard Increase camber (and on Fowler flaps, wing area). More lift and more drag at the same airspeed, so the aircraft flies slower and descends steeper. Lower stall speed. Landing approach in stages. Short-field takeoff (partial flap). Slow flight. Almost every aircraft. 172: yes (electric, 0-10-20-30°).
Slatsleading edge, movable Extend forward and down to open a gap at the leading edge. High-energy air from below flows over the top of the wing and keeps it attached at a higher angle of attack. Raises the stalling angle, so stall speed drops. Takeoff and landing, usually in step with the flaps. Automatic on some light aircraft at high angle of attack. Airliners and jets, STOL types. 172: no.
Slotsleading edge, fixed gap Same job as a slat, done with a permanent opening through the wing near the leading edge. Delays the stall over that part of the wing. Often placed outboard so the ailerons keep working while the inboard wing stalls first. Always working; nothing for the pilot to operate. Matters most at high angle of attack: slow flight, approach, stall recovery. STOL and bush aircraft, some trainers. 172: no (the 172 uses wing twist and stall strips instead).
Spoilersupper wing surface Hinge up into the airflow on top of the wing and destroy lift on that wing. Add drag. Used one side for roll (no adverse yaw), both sides to descend without gaining speed, and fully deployed on touchdown to put weight on the wheels. Gliders: every approach. Jets: roll assist, steep descents, and "ground spoilers" the moment the wheels touch. Gliders, jets, some turboprops. 172: no.
Speed brakesfuselage or wing panels Add drag only, with little change in lift. Let the aircraft slow down or descend steeply while keeping the wing flying normally. Spoilers can double as speed brakes; true speed brakes are separate panels. Descent from altitude, slowing to approach speed, keeping speed in check in a dive or steep descent. Military and jet aircraft use them constantly. Jets, military aircraft, some fast singles (Mooney, Cirrus option). 172: no.
Rule of thumb Flaps, slats and slots add lift so you can fly slower. Spoilers kill lift. Speed brakes add drag. Every one of them is about managing lift and drag; none of them moves the aircraft about an axis the way a primary control does.
Drawing is schematic. The 172 silhouette is a stock Skyhawk; slats, slots, spoilers and speed brakes are placed where other aircraft carry them.

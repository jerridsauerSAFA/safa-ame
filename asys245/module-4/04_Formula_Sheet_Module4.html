Tabs — which way do they move?

TabMoves…PurposeFound on
Trim tabOpposite to the surfaceHold the surface → hands-off flightElevator (most), rudder, ailerons
Servo tabOpposite, driven by the pilotAir helps move a heavy surfaceBig aircraft without hydraulic boost
Balance tabOpposite, linked to the surfaceLighten control forcesAilerons, elevators
Anti-servo tabSame direction as the surfaceAdd feel, stop over-controllingStabilators (also the trim tab)
Fixed tabBent on the groundCure a wing- or nose-heavy tendencyLight aircraft ailerons and rudders

Memory line: every tab moves opposite to help or hold — except the anti-servo tab, which moves the same way to resist.

Two quick calculators

Why a tiny tab works

Tab moment

M = F × d
Mturning moment on the control surfaceFair force on the tabddistance from the tab to the surface hinge

A small force far behind the hinge makes as much turning effect as a big force close to it. That is why a trim tab a few inches wide can hold a whole elevator.

Same formula as Module 3's moment — the tab is just a second, smaller lever riding on the first.

Worked example

A 4 lb air load on a tab 14 in behind the elevator hinge: M = 4 × 14 = 56 lb·in. To make the same moment with a pilot force applied 2 in from the hinge you would need 56 ÷ 2 = 28 lb.

M = 56 lb·in
Temperature correction

Cable tension vs temperature

Tset = Tchart ± Δ
Tcharttension the manual gives at the reference temperature (often 70 °F / 21 °C)Δcorrection from the chart for today's hangar temperature

Aluminum structure grows and shrinks more than the steel cable. Cold hangar → cable reads tighter; hot → looser. Always set tension using the chart for the temperature you are working in.

Never carry a remembered "40 lb" from one aircraft to another — the number belongs to the type and the chart.

Worked example

A chart says 40 lb at 70 °F and the correction line shows about −0.3 lb per °F below reference. In a 40 °F hangar (30 °F colder): 40 − 0.3 × 30 = 31 lb on the tensiometer today, so that it reads 40 lb in normal conditions. (Illustrative slope — read the real chart.)

Set to 31 lb

After any flight-control work

StepWhat you confirm
1. RigNeutral positions with rigging pins; travel with a protractor; tension with the tensiometer and chart
2. SecureTurnbuckles safetied, jam nuts tight, check holes pass the wire test (wire must NOT go through), pulley guards on
3. Control checkCorrect direction · full travel to the stops · free and smooth · no fouling at full deflection
4. Independent inspectionSecond qualified person inspects and signs — CAR 571.10
